Research and Modeling

This research applies Extreme Value Theory (EVT) to both corporate and municipal financial data. Utilizing the Generalized Extreme Value (GEV) distribution and the Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD), we rigorously model the behavior of rare but significant financial events, such as extreme declines in stock returns or municipal revenue. This approach enables a more accurate assessment of tail risks, capturing the probability and magnitude of extreme outcomes that lie beyond typical financial fluctuations.

Finding Distributions That Make Sense For Our Data

Before developing our tailored model, an essential first step is to establish an appropriate baseline model by identifying one or more probability distributions that best describe the underlying structure and variability within the data. This initial understanding will guide the subsequent development and refinement of a model optimized to explain municipal decline in its specific context.

Given our focus on modeling extreme events, we must move beyond general distributions, such as the Normal distribution, which are known to significantly underestimate rare shocks and financial bankruptcies. Instead, our initial analysis focuses on distributions specifically designed to capture the behavior of extremes, including:

  • Generalized Pareto Distribution with Peaks Over Threshold
  • Burr Distribution
  • Generalized Extreme Value Distribution with Block Maxima
  • Log-Normal Distribution
  • Weibull Distribution
  • Marshall-Olkin Transformation

The Probability Density Functions (PDFs) of these distributions are crucial. They describe how likely different values of x are to occur, and the area under each curve represents total probability. These functions are critical because they determine the shape and heaviness of the tails of extreme events in our data, directly influencing our ultimate risk assessments.
